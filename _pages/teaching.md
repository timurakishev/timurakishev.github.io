Teaching materials, course resources, and other useful things from my classes.

I currently teach the following linguistics courses at [KIMEP University](https://www.kimep.kz/) in Almaty, Kazakhstan.

### [fall 2026]

**LING3306 — Corpus Linguistics** 📚
A practical introduction to corpus linguistics, focusing on the analysis of real-world language data using contemporary corpus tools and methods. Students explore corpus compilation, concordancing, frequency analysis, collocations, and other quantitative and qualitative approaches to discovering linguistic patterns.

**LING2101 — Language and Its Structure I: Phonetics and Phonology** 👂
An introduction to the physical properties of speech sounds and the phonological patterns and processes underlying language. Topics include speech production and perception, phonetic transcription using the IPA, acoustic analysis, syllable structure, and phonological rules.

**LING2201 — Language and Its Structure II: Morphology and Syntax** 🌳
An introduction to the internal structure of words and the principles governing the formation of phrases and sentences. Topics include morphemes, word formation, constituency, structural relations, X-bar theory, syntactic movement, and generative approaches to syntax.

### [tools & software]
I incorporate a variety of linguistic software and computational tools into my teaching and research.

- 📚 **Corpus linguistics & NLP:** [#LancsBox X](https://lancsbox.lancs.ac.uk/), [AntConc](https://www.laurenceanthony.net/software/antconc/), and custom Python scripts for basic natural language processing and corpus analysis.
- 👂 **Phonetics:** [Praat](https://praat.org/) for acoustic speech analysis and [R](https://www.r-project.org/) for data visualization.
- 🌳 **Syntax:** Online syntax tree generators, [TreeForm](https://sourceforge.net/projects/treeform/), and LaTeX for constructing and visualizing syntactic structures.

### [in development]
🌳 **Syntax Tree Builder**
I'm currently developing my own application for constructing syntax trees. While existing tools such as TreeForm are useful, many have outdated interfaces and limited flexibility. My goal is to create a more modern, intuitive tool for teaching and practicing syntactic analysis.
