Teaching materials, course resources, and other useful things from my classes.

I currently teach the following linguistics courses at KIMEP University in Almaty, Kazakhstan.

### [fall 2026]

**LING3306 — Corpus Linguistics**. This course offers a practical introduction to corpus linguistics. Students explore a range of traditional and contemporary tools and methods for analyzing real-world language data, developing the skills to identify linguistic patterns and trends in corpora and draw evidence-based conclusions about language use.

**LING2101 — Language and Its Structure I: Phonetics and Phonology**. This course offers an introduction to phonetics and phonology. Students explore the physical properties and linguistic characteristics of speech sounds, developing an understanding of how these sounds are organized into complex phonological patterns and systems and how they interact in phonological processes.

**LING2201 — Language and Its Structure II: Morphology and Syntax**. This course introduces morphology and syntax, focusing on how words are formed and organized into phrases and sentences. Students explore word structure and formation processes, along with fundamental concepts in generative syntax, including constituency, structural relations, binding, X-bar theory, movement, feature checking, and minimalism.

### [tools & software]
I use a variety of linguistic software and computational tools in my teaching and research.

- 📚 **Corpus linguistics & NLP:** [#LancsBox X](https://lancsbox.lancs.ac.uk/), [AntConc](https://www.laurenceanthony.net/software/antconc/), and custom Python scripts in [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/) for basic natural language processing and corpus compilation/analysis.
- 👂 **Phonetics:** [Praat](https://praat.org/) for acoustic speech analysis and [RStudio](https://posit.co/downloads) for data visualization.
- 🌳 **Syntax:** Online syntax tree generators and LaTeX ([Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/)) for constructing and visualizing syntactic structures.

### [apps I've already built]
I've developed several small applications and games for my Phonetics and Phonology course to make linguistic analysis and practice more interactive.

#### 1. Guess the Accent 🌍
**Guess the Accent** is a listening game in which students hear recordings of speakers reading the same English passage and try to identify their accents. The recordings and reading passage come from the [Speech Accent Archive](https://accent.gmu.edu/) at George Mason University, a great resource for exploring accent variation in English. My students really enjoyed playing it! The current version includes 15 questions, and I'm planning to add more recordings and give the interface a more modern look. For now, it's a work in progress, but I'm happy to share the 35 MB application for free, non-commercial educational use upon request. I may develop a licensed version in the future.
