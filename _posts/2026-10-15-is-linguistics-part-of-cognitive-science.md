Some of my students think I'm a Translation Studies professor. Others don't seem to realize that linguistics is part of cognitive science. Both make me wonder what students actually think linguistics is.

In my Corpus Linguistics course, I teach students to build and analyze corpora using different software tools and Python libraries. A lot of this work involves NLP, especially in contemporary corpus linguistics. But linguistics isn't just about computational tools, either. It's also about understanding how language works, from speech sounds and syntactic structures to meaning and language use.

What puzzles me is that some Cognitive Science students don't see the connection. I've even had students suggest in their final course evaluations that semantics and logic, which I teach in my Logic of Language course, have little to do with their major.

But how can we study human cognition without studying language? Even Andrew Carnie discusses syntax as part of cognitive science in his *Syntax: A Generative Introduction*.

I don't expect everyone to be fascinated by linguistics. Still, I find it strange that students can spend semesters studying language and not necessarily recognize its place in the broader study of the mind.

Maybe we need to do a better job of showing how these disciplines connect. Or maybe I just think about linguistics too much.
